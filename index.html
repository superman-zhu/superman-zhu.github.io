  1. Course Project

    NYC Airbnb Compliance Analysis: Detecting “Shadow Listings” under Local Law 18

    TECH-UB 57 Data Science and AI for Business, NYU Stern · Prof. Chris Volinsky Spring 2026

    New York's Local Law 18 is enforced reactively, and the hosts most worth catching are the ones who edit their listing fields to look compliant. Working from 36,111 Inside Airbnb listings, we built a rule-based proxy label for illegality and trained six classifiers to rank the surface-compliant pool by violation risk; Random Forest reached a test AUC of 0.9892 and was chosen over a marginally better soft-voting ensemble for its SHAP interpretability. A Monte Carlo simulation of audit outcomes, paired with a cost-benefit matrix drawn from the LL18 fine schedule, showed that under a realistic 1,000-inspection budget the model-driven audit list hits violations 82.8% of the time against 9.1% for random selection — an incremental $1.84M per cycle. Delivered with a Streamlit dashboard for OSE field dispatch.

    Group project with Tanzim Ali, Josh Kwon, and Ziyu Tian.

  2. Case Report

    Forecasting the Invesco QQQ Trust with an ARIMA–GARCH Model

    STAT-GB 6018 Forecasting Time Series Data, NYU Stern · Prof. Avi Giloni Fall 2025

    Fits an ARIMA(2,1,2) model to five years of daily log adjusted close prices for QQQ, then shows that although the residuals are uncorrelated they are not independent — the squared residuals remain significantly autocorrelated. Adding a GARCH(1,1) layer captures the volatility clustering and yields a narrower one-step-ahead 95% forecast interval than the ARIMA-only model, with an empirical failure rate of 5.1% against a nominal 5%. A normal probability plot of the standardised residuals shows the model still does not fully absorb the leptokurtosis in the data.

  3. Case Report

    An ARIMA Model for U.S. Total Consumer Credit, 1967–2025

    STAT-GB 6018 Forecasting Time Series Data, NYU Stern · Prof. Avi Giloni Fall 2025

    Models 58 years of monthly FRED data on total consumer credit owned and securitized. A log transform linearises the exponential growth and a single difference achieves stationarity; AICc selects ARIMA(1,1,1) with a constant, whose parameters are all significant at the 5% level and whose residuals pass Ljung–Box tests at every lag examined. The fitted model forecasts 150 months forward with 95% intervals that widen as expected with the horizon.

  4. Case Report

    Ford's Value Enhancement Plan: Returning $10 Billion Without Losing Control

    BUSF-SHU 303 Corporate Finance, NYU Shanghai · Prof. Guodong Chen Spring 2025

    By 2000 Ford was sitting on $23 billion in cash — roughly 40% of its market capitalization — with a lagging stock price. We argue the payout was justified, compare it against dividends and open-market repurchases, and work through why the VEP's one-for-one exchange plus a $20 cash-or-stock election served long-term holders better than a buyback would have. The plan delivered on value and flexibility, but we side with TIAA-CREF and CalPERS on governance: preserving the Ford family's 40% voting power while its economic stake fell toward 3.6% is hard to defend on one-share-one-vote grounds.

  5. Case Report

    MCI Communications: Financing a Challenger, 1972–1988

    BUSF-SHU 303 Corporate Finance, NYU Shanghai · Prof. Guodong Chen Spring 2025

    Traces MCI's financing through three phases — equity for survival, lease financing to work around loan covenants, then convertibles once the Execunet restrictions lifted — and shows each choice following from the constraint the firm faced at the time. Building the funds-flow forecast from the case exhibits puts external financing needs for FY84–88 near $3.5 billion, varying roughly ±30% under our good and bad scenarios. Of the CFO's five options we recommend the $600 million 7.625% convertible debenture: the $54 conversion price sits above the $47 share price, so it raises capital now and defers dilution until the stock justifies it.

  6. Case Report

    American Chemical Corporation: Valuing the Collinsville Plant

    BUSF-SHU 303 Corporate Finance, NYU Shanghai · Prof. Guodong Chen Spring 2025

    Identifies Pennwalt and Kerr-McGee as the closest comparables to the Collinsville sodium chlorate plant, unlevers their equity betas to an asset beta of 0.899, and arrives at a 15.79% discount rate under CAPM. On those terms the plant is worth −$3.80 million without the laminate technology and marginally positive with it — a result thin enough that the recommendation turns on judgment rather than the number. We argue against the purchase: the projections assume a single product, a single regional market, and power costs at 55–60% of manufacturing expense, none of which the comparables share.

    With Henry He.

  1. Internship Project

    Reproducing a GRU-Based Deep Learning Stock Selection Model

    Summer Internship, Fullgoal Fund Management Co.,ltd. Summer 2026

    Reproduces a sell-side quant research report on combining long/short return signals with market-regime modeling in a GRU-based stock selection framework. Given the data constraints of an individual reproduction relative to the original report (2010-2026, full A-share universe, 38 high-frequency features), this version works with a substantially reduced dataset: CSI 300 constituents (~290 stocks), roughly 2 years of daily data, and a minute-bar feature set covering only 6 sample stocks over ~6 months.

    Model construction follows the report's full progression: a baseline single-path GRU on daily price/volume-derived features, a multi-task variant with a 5-day auxiliary head reconciled via PCGrad gradient surgery, and a path-supervised variant that adds step-wise return targets across the input window. Layers on two market-regime branches — an endogenous branch that infers latent regimes from the cross-sectional structure of stock representations and residualizes out common-state exposure via a mixture-of-experts head, and an external branch that conditions stock representations on index-level market data through FiLM modulation — plus a combined variant merging both. Each stage is evaluated with Rank IC and decile long-short portfolio tests. The high-frequency demo dataset is used separately to validate the incremental value of a dual-path (low-frequency + high-frequency) GRU extension.

    Note that the notebook is written in Chinese.

  4. Course Project

    ReadRadar: An ML-Powered Book Discovery App

    CSCI-UA 473 Fundamentals of Machine Learning, New York University · Prof. Kyunghyun Cho Spring 2026

    A Streamlit app built on the UCSD Book Graph that lets readers search a 5,000-book catalog by theme, browse it as clusters, and get recommendations from what they have saved. Semantic search runs on Nomic v1.5 embeddings truncated to 384 dimensions; recommendations build a recency-weighted persona vector from the favorites list so newer picks pull the results harder; and the catalog is grouped into 24 "Reading Neighborhoods" by spherical k-means++. Retrieval, recommendation scoring, and clustering are all implemented directly in NumPy rather than called from a library. A separate Claude-backed pipeline reads twelve rating-balanced reviews per book and returns grounded controversy tags and a short reader verdict.

    Group project with Krystal Wu, Bruce Wang, Benji Hua, and Yizhen.

  5. Course Project

    PivotPoint AI: Office-to-Residential Conversion Feasibility Scoring

    FINC-GB 2328 Data Science and Artificial Intelligence in Real Estate, NYU Stern · Prof. Sam Chandan Spring 2026

    Upload a commercial floor plan and an address; PivotPoint returns a 0–100 feasibility score for converting the building to residential use, with violations drawn directly onto the plan and the relevant NYC building code cited alongside. Gemini 2.5 Pro reads the plan for window-to-wall ratio, aspect ratio, and floor-plate depth, but never scores — the score comes from a deterministic 14-subscore formula over PLUTO zoning, landmark, and transit-distance data, so the same building always returns the same number. Code citations are retrieved from a pgvector store of NYC regulations, and the output is packaged as a deal memo an analyst can hand to a client.

    Group project with Skye Xi, Leo Lei, Patrick Suter, and Julia Niehaus.

  6. Course Project

    Chat & Play: A LAN Chat Platform with Encryption and a Two-Player Game

    CSCI-SHU 101 Introduction to Computer and Data Science, NYU Shanghai · Mingyuan Wang Fall 2024

    One server, many clients: register, log in, and chat one-on-one over a custom length-prefixed TCP protocol, with the server multiplexing every socket through select(). Messages in a private chat are encrypted with an RSA keypair generated per exchange — key generation, encryption, and decryption written from scratch rather than pulled from a library. The server indexes every message per user so history is searchable, and a Tkinter front end adds Shakespeare sonnet lookup and a two-player Pygame Snake match you can start from the chat window.
